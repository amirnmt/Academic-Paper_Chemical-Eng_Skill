# Vocabulary by section and function

Word lists for building sentences in each part of an engineering research article. They are organized by what the words *do*. Choose the word whose meaning and strength match your evidence, and check collocations (which words go together) in papers from your field or a collocation dictionary.

Sentence stems that use these words are in `phrasebank.md`; the Academic Word List is described in `academic-vocabulary.md`.

Best practice: build your own list from your field. For each section, collect the words and phrases that recur in 15–30 recent papers from your target journal (`scripts/build_section_phrases.py` does this for a folder of converted papers) and use them as your first choice.

## 1. Linking words (signalling the relation between sentences)

| Function | Words | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Addition | in addition, moreover, furthermore, also, as well as, besides (persuasive) | Use only when the second point really adds to the first |
| Cause | because, as, since, owing to, due to, on account of, in view of | "since" can also mean "from that time"; avoid it if ambiguous |
| Result | therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence, which is why, so that | Do not start a sentence with "So" |
| Contrast (difference) | however, whereas, while, by contrast, in contrast, on the other hand, but | "while" can also mean "at the same time" |
| Opposite | conversely, on the contrary | Only when the two things are opposites, not merely different |
| Concession (unexpected) | although, even though, though; despite, in spite of, regardless of, notwithstanding; nevertheless, nonetheless, yet, even so | despite + noun; although + clause |
| Similarity | similarly, likewise, in the same way, correspondingly | |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as, in particular, e.g. (in brackets only) | "such as" before a non-exhaustive list |
| Clarification | that is, in other words, namely, i.e. (in brackets only) | |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, as long as, in the case of | |
| Purpose | to, in order to, so as to, for the purpose of, with the aim of | |
| Sequence | first, second, then, subsequently, finally | Avoid numbering more than three or four steps in prose; use a list or table |
| Summary | in summary, overall, taken together, in short | Once per section at most |
| Generalization | in general, generally, on the whole, in most cases, as a rule | |

## 2. Introduction

**Establishing importance and background**
- Nouns: importance, role, relevance, interest, demand, application, use, need, concern, challenge, limitation, requirement, development, advance.
- Adjectives (use with evidence): important, widespread, common, central, essential, fundamental, major, increasing, growing, considerable, economic, industrial, practical, efficient, energy-intensive, exothermic, large-scale.
- Verbs and phrases: is widely used in, is one of the main routes to, is essential for, accounts for, plays a [specific] part in (state the part), has attracted attention because, has been studied extensively, is a key step in (only if it is).
- Time frames: in recent years, over the past decade, since the 1990s, currently, to date, at present.

**Presenting previous research (reporting verbs)**
- Neutral: describe, report, present, examine, investigate, study, measure, model, simulate, analyse, compare, consider, apply, use, develop, propose.
- Showing the result: show, demonstrate, find, observe, identify, establish, confirm, reveal, determine, obtain, predict.
- Tentative (the source was cautious): suggest, propose, indicate, hypothesize, argue.
- Distancing (you may disagree): claim, assume, assert.
- Tense: past simple for a specific study ("Smith et al. [4] measured …"); present perfect for a line of research continuing now ("Several authors have modelled …"); present simple for accepted findings ("Cu/ZnO catalysts deactivate by sintering [7]").
- Most frequent after named studies in *Computers & Chemical Engineering* Introductions [J-obs]: used, proposed, developed, indicated, suggested, improved, introduced, showed, combined, found, employed; also built, examined, addressed, analysed, applied, compared, derived, highlighted, extended.

**Identifying the gap or problem**
- Signals: however, although, while, despite, nevertheless, but, yet.
- Adjectives: limited, restricted (to), incomplete, inconsistent, contradictory, inconclusive, unclear, uncertain, inaccurate, insufficient, inadequate, oversimplified, time-consuming, computationally expensive, impractical, unvalidated, not well understood.
- Nouns: gap, lack, absence, limitation, drawback, disadvantage, shortcoming, problem, difficulty, obstacle, need, uncertainty, discrepancy, inconsistency, controversy.
- Verbs: neglect, overlook, ignore (use carefully), fail to account for, be restricted to, remain unclear, remain unknown, require further investigation, has not been examined/validated/reported.
- Quantifiers: few, little (= almost none), only a few, no study, to our knowledge no.
  Note: "a little" means "some"; "little" means "almost none".

**Linking the gap to the present work:** to address these limitations, to this end, accordingly, therefore, against this background, in this context, these limitations motivate, building on.

**Stating scope:** is limited to, lies beyond the scope of, within the investigated range, does not describe, is not intended to, is left for future work.

**Presenting the present work**
- Nouns: aim, objective, purpose, goal, contribution, approach, scope, focus.
- Verbs: present, propose, develop, introduce, describe, examine, investigate, quantify, evaluate, compare, validate, extend, apply, demonstrate, assess, determine.
- Use the precise verb. Not every section "discusses"; some compare, analyse, derive or validate.

## 3. Literature review

**Grouping and synthesizing:** approaches, groups, categories, strategies, families of models, lines of research; can be divided into, fall into, can be distinguished, broadly, mainly, in particular, similarly, in contrast.

**Comparing studies:** consistent with, in agreement with, similar to, comparable to, analogous to, in line with, differ from, in contrast to, unlike, contradict, extend, build on, refine, generalize, complement.

**Evaluating studies (constructive, not insulting):** limited to, restricted to, did not consider, did not include, assumed, relied on, was validated only for, applies only to, valid for, captures … but neglects, provides a useful basis for, offers an improvement over. Avoid "failed to", "ignored", "overlooked" about named authors; prefer "did not examine".

**Quantity of evidence:** a single study, few studies, several studies, a number of studies, a large body of work, extensive literature, most studies, the majority of.

## 4. Methods

**Overview and source**
- obtained from, supplied by, purchased from, provided by, used as received, prepared by/according to, synthesized by, collected from, digitized from, taken from.
- carried out, conducted, performed, undertaken, implemented, set up.

**Specific action verbs (instead of "done" or "used")**
- Preparation: prepared, synthesized, calcined, reduced, activated, pretreated, diluted, mixed, sieved, pelletized, crushed.
- Measurement: measured, recorded, monitored, sampled, analysed, quantified, characterized, detected, determined, calibrated.
- Modelling and computation: modelled, simulated, discretized, integrated, solved, linearized, approximated, interpolated, extrapolated, fitted, regressed, estimated, optimized, parameterized, implemented, coded, validated, verified, initialized, converged.
- Data processing: filtered, smoothed, normalized, scaled, averaged, aggregated, resampled, split, trained, tested, cross-validated, tuned.
- Arrangement: assembled, installed, mounted, connected, positioned, aligned, inserted, embedded, insulated.

**Equipment, geometry and location**
- upstream of, downstream of, at the inlet/outlet, along the axis, at the centreline, near the wall, in the radial direction, adjacent to, parallel to, perpendicular to, coaxial with, concentric, equally spaced, at intervals of, mounted on, fitted with, embedded in, surrounded by, connected to, aligned with, located at, positioned at.
- Precise proximity: immediately/directly/just above vs slightly above; flush with, in contact with.
- Size expressions: 20 mm long = with a length of 20 mm; twice as long as = twice the length of; half as wide as = half the width of.

**Justifying choices**
- in order to, so as to, to (+ verb), for the purpose of, with the aim of, in an attempt to, to take advantage of, for the sake of simplicity, to ensure that, to avoid, to minimize, to eliminate, to account for.
- chosen because, selected on the basis of, chosen for its, which allowed/enabled/ensured, thereby + -ing, by + -ing, it was possible to, this approach avoids, the advantage of X is that.
- Verbs of purpose: achieve, allow, enable, ensure, facilitate, avoid, prevent, reduce, minimize, compensate for, account for, confirm, validate, verify, establish, identify, improve, overcome, provide, remove.

**Showing that care was taken**
- carefully, accurately, precisely, rigorously, thoroughly, systematically, independently, repeatedly, consistently, uniformly, randomly, separately, immediately, continuously, at least three times, in triplicate, under identical conditions, within ±0.5 K, to within 1 %.

**Relating your method to others**
- Same: as described in [n], according to [n], following [n], using the method of [n], as reported previously [n], in accordance with, details are given in [n].
- Similar: a modified version of, adapted from, based on, with minor modifications, essentially the same as, slightly modified.
- Different (state the change): loosely based on, partly based on, with the following modifications, extended to, in contrast to [n], a new step was added.
- Name the method even when citing it: "the catalyst was reduced in diluted H2 as described in [8]", not "as described in [8]" alone.

**Models and equations**
- Lead-ins to displayed equations, by frequency in C&CE Methodology sections [J-obs]: defined as, obtained as/by, calculated as/from, as follows, expressed as, computed as, given by, formulated as, reads, is described by, is written as.
- Symbols after the equation: where … is/are, denotes, represents, stands for, refers to, corresponds to; let … denote.
- Linking equations: substituting … into, inserting, combining, using Eq. (…), from Eqs. (…)–(…), reduces to, simplifies to, yields, gives, it follows that, is coupled with/through, is closed by, subject to.
- Status of a model element: adopted from, follows, is taken from, adapted from, extended to, modified by adding, combined with, introduced here, proposed in this work.

**Assumptions:** is assumed (to be), is considered, is taken as, is neglected, is negligible, is lumped, is approximated by, is treated as, is idealized as, for simplicity, to keep the problem tractable, holds for, is valid when, is justified by, may lead to, overestimates, underestimates.

**Numerics:** discretized, collocation points, finite volumes, method of lines, stiff, implicit, explicit, integrated, solved, converged, tolerance, optimality gap, warm start, initialized, sequential, simultaneous, decomposition, reformulated, relaxed, independence, refinement.

**Data-driven methods:** curated, synchronized, aggregated, filtered, imputed, normalized, scaled on the training set, split chronologically, held out, leakage, trained, validated, tuned, early stopping, seed, ensemble, baseline, ablation.

**Reporting problems in the procedure**
- Minimizing the problem honestly: slight, small, minor, negligible, only approximate, within the measurement uncertainty, did not affect the results (state why).
- Explaining it: inevitably, unavoidable, limited by, it was not possible to, could not be measured, difficult to control, beyond the scope of this study.
- Positive framing (only if true): acceptable, sufficient for the purpose, reasonably robust.
- Solution: future work will (your plan) / future work should (invitation to others); care should be taken to.

## 5. Results

**Referring to the aim or method**
- To examine/test/quantify …, we …; as described in Section 2.3; as predicted; in order to …; the aim of this analysis was to.

**Pointing to figures and tables** (verbs for the figure as subject): shows, presents, compares, summarizes, illustrates, plots, lists, depicts, gives, reveals.
- "from Fig. 3" = deduced from it; "in Fig. 3" = appears in it.
- Prefer the finding as subject with the figure in brackets: "The hot spot shifted downstream (Fig. 3)."

**Describing change and trends**
- Increase: increase, rise, grow, climb, reach a maximum, peak, double, exceed.
- Decrease: decrease, fall, decline, drop, reduce, diminish, reach a minimum, halve.
- No change: remain constant, remain unchanged, level off, stabilize, plateau, saturate.
- Variation: vary, fluctuate, oscillate, scatter, deviate.
- Rate and shape: sharply, steeply, rapidly, gradually, steadily, slightly, linearly, exponentially, monotonically, asymptotically, non-linearly.
- Relationship: is proportional to, scales with, correlates with, is independent of, depends on, is sensitive/insensitive to.

**Objective vs evaluative description**
- Objective: higher, lower, increased, decreased, unchanged, peaked at, by 12 %, from 480 to 510 K.
- Evaluative add-on (choose to match the numbers): slightly, considerably, markedly, substantially, sharply, negligibly; good, excellent, satisfactory, poor; consistent, comparable, similar.
- Published results usually combine both: "slightly lower (by 2 K)".

**Frequency** (from always to never): always, invariably, in all cases; generally, usually, normally; regularly, repeatedly; frequently, often, commonly; more often than not; as often as not; sometimes, at times; occasionally; rarely, seldom; hardly ever, almost never; never, in no case. Prefer counts: "in 9 of 12 runs".

**Quantity**
- Make it look large: considerable, substantial, marked, as many as, as much as, over half, upwards of, at least, the majority of.
- Make it look small: only, merely, barely, as few as, negligible, slight, marginal, a small fraction of.
- Emphasize: by far, well above/below, far exceeding, even higher/lower, considerably.
- Close to a value: approximately, nearly, almost, virtually, close to, just over/under.
- Neutral (no commitment): some, a number of, relatively, moderately, to some extent.
Pair every such word with the number.

**Sequence and time**
- Before: beforehand, previously, prior to, initially.
- Start: at the start, at t = 0, initially, at first.
- Order: then, next, subsequently, after, following.
- Short gap: soon after, shortly after, immediately, within [t].
- Long gap: later, eventually, after [t], towards the end.
- Simultaneous: while, as, during, at the same time, simultaneously, upon + -ing.
- End and after: finally, at the end of, afterwards, thereafter.

**Agreement with models, validation data and other studies**
- Good: agrees well with, is in good agreement with, closely matches, reproduces, is consistent with, lies within the experimental uncertainty, confirms, corroborates, validates.
- Partial: reproduces the trend but, agrees qualitatively, agrees within [x] % except, is of the same order as.
- Poor: deviates from, overpredicts, underpredicts, overestimates, underestimates, departs from, is inconsistent with, contradicts.

**Certainty and scope** (see `results-discussion-writing.md` §19): shows, demonstrates (controlled evidence); indicates, confirms within, is consistent with (strong); suggests, is likely to, can be attributed to (moderate); may, might, a possible explanation is (weak); within the investigated range, under the considered assumptions, for this data set (scope); does not establish, does not show (negative).
- "Significant" only with a statistical test and its level; otherwise large, substantial, clear, or the number. In 20 C&CE papers, "significant(ly)" appeared in 62 sentences, only one of which reported a statistical quantity [J-obs].

**Validation and benchmarking:** fits (calibration data), predicts (independent data), reproduces, captures the trend, underpredicts, overpredicts, bias, scatter, maximum deviation, under identical inputs, on the same split, against the nominal model, held-out, independent campaign.

**Computational performance:** per evaluation, wall-clock time, speed-up, -fold, scales with, bottleneck, at equal tolerance, on the same hardware.

**Problems with results**
- small number of outliers, a slight mismatch, within the measurement uncertainty, scatter, noise, drift, numerical artefact, possible source of error, could not be resolved, beyond the scope of this study.

**Transition to interpretation (end of Results)**
- suggests that, indicates that, implies that, points to, is consistent with the hypothesis that, may be explained by, could be attributed to.

## 6. Discussion

**Revisiting findings:** repeat the key terms of the objective exactly (an "echo"); the main finding, the results show, this study has shown.

**Explaining (cause and mechanism)**
- Strong (direct evidence): causes, leads to, results in, gives rise to, is due to, is caused by, produces, determines, controls.
- Partial: contributes to, is a factor in, plays a part in, partly explains, influences, affects.
- First cause: originates in, stems from, arises from, is triggered by, initiates.
- Weak (link, direction uncertain): is associated with, is related to, is linked to, correlates with, coincides with, accompanies.
- Grammar: "X results in Y" (X = cause); "X results from Y" (Y = cause); "a cause of" (one of several) vs "the cause of" (the only one); attribute/ascribe X to Y.

**Mapping your work onto the literature**
- Agreement: consistent with, in agreement with, confirms, supports, corroborates, mirrors, parallels, reproduces.
- Extension: extends, broadens, generalizes, refines, complements, provides insight into, builds on.
- Disagreement: differs from, contrasts with, contradicts, challenges, does not support, is at odds with.
- Comparison with comparatives: more accurate than, faster than, lower error than, at a fraction of the computational cost of.

**Stating the contribution (use only with evidence)**
- Neutral-positive: accurate, reliable, efficient, feasible, practical, straightforward, cost-effective, general, transferable, valid, consistent.
- Strong (rarely, when the data justify it): novel, first, substantially, markedly, considerably.
- Verbs: enables, allows, provides the first, demonstrates the feasibility of, removes the need for, reduces, outperforms, rules out, offers a basis for.
- Avoid decorative intensifiers (crucial, pivotal, remarkable, groundbreaking); see `avoid-ai-signs.md`.

**Hedging (certainty scale)**
- Certain (direct evidence): no modal; shows, demonstrates.
- Virtually certain, not proven: must, must have; cannot have (= virtually impossible).
- Probable: is likely to, probably, should (= expected), it is likely that.
- Possible: may, might, could, can, possibly, perhaps, a possible explanation is.
- Hedging verbs: suggest, indicate, appear, seem, tend to.
- Limiting phrases: under the conditions studied, within the investigated range, for this reactor, to some extent, in most cases.
- Boosters (direct evidence only): clearly, demonstrates, confirms, it is evident that.

**Limitations and future work**
- one limitation, a limitation of this approach, is restricted to, does not account for, assumes, was validated only for, should be interpreted with caution, the effect of … was not examined.
- future work will (your own plan), future work should/could (invitation), remains to be determined, warrants further investigation, should be validated/tested/extended.

**Applications**
- can be applied to, could be used for, is suitable for, has potential for, makes it possible to, should allow, could support the design of.

## 7. Conclusion

- This study has shown/demonstrated that; the main conclusions are; the results indicate; these findings provide; the proposed approach enables; future work will.
- Tense: present perfect for what the study achieved ("has shown"), past for what was done and found, present for what the findings mean.

## 8. Abstract

- Background/problem: present simple ("X is …; however, Y remains …").
- What the paper does: present simple ("This paper presents …"; "We compare …").
- Methods: past simple passive ("was modelled", "were measured") or present simple for analyses in the paper ("is validated against").
- Results: past simple ("decreased by", "was found"), or present simple to state them directly.
- Achievement: present perfect ("We have shown …") or present simple ("The model predicts …").
- Implications and applications: present simple with can/may ("can be applied to"); avoid weak forms like "seem to suggest" in the abstract.

## 10. Commonly confused words

- affect (verb) / effect (noun; as a verb "effect" = bring about).
- amount (uncountable) / number (countable); less (uncountable) / fewer (countable).
- varying (changing) / various (different).
- which (adds information, with commas) / that (defines, no commas): "catalysts, which deactivate quickly, …" (all do) vs "catalysts that deactivate quickly …" (only some).
- i.e. (that is) / e.g. (for example).
- compared with (to show differences) / compared to (to liken).
- continuous (without interruption) / continual (repeated).
- principle (a rule) / principal (main).
- data, criteria, phenomena, spectra, maxima, minima, formulae are plural; datum, criterion, phenomenon, spectrum, maximum, minimum, formula singular (follow the journal for "data is/are").
- "10 g was added" (one quantity) vs "10 g were added" (in portions).
